Historical Introduction: The Birth of a Visual Identity

Examining the national symbols of the United States is not merely a review of graphic designs or flora and fauna, but a profound journey into the cultural, political, and social DNA of the nation. From its birth in the storm of the Revolutionary War (1775–1783), the United States faced an enormous challenge: how to unite thirteen disparate colonies, with different economic interests and diverse demographic backgrounds, into a single republic under a shared national identity?

The answer lies largely in its symbols. The Founding Fathers understood that to forge loyalty to a new country lacking a monarch or centuries-old history like European nations, they needed to create a system of visual symbols that would act as a cultural glue. These symbols were designed to reflect ideals of liberty, justice, power, independence, and unity.

From the Great Seal, whose design took six long years and three different committees to achieve the required symbolic precision, to the decision to adopt the Bald Eagle as the symbol of power and freedom despite certain objections (such as the famous one by Benjamin Franklin). Every symbol holds stories of individuals, ideological struggles, and fateful decisions. This project invites you to explore the history, exact etymology, and identity behind each symbol, and to understand how "Out of Many, One" (E Pluribus Unum) transitioned from a coined phrase to a national reality.
