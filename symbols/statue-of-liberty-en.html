The Statue of Liberty

1886 | Beacon of Immigration & Freedom

HE

Statue of Liberty

From a magnificent French gift to becoming the universal symbol of hope, immigration, and the promise of liberty in the New World.

1. Historical Identity: A Gift Across the Ocean

  • Year of Dedication: October 28, 1886.
  • Creators: The French sculptor Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi (design and sculpting), and the engineer Gustave Eiffel (design of the internal skeleton). It was clarified that the sculptor was the French sculptor Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi.
  • Reason for Construction: The statue was given as a gift from the French people to the American people, initiated by the French politician Édouard de Laboulaye, to celebrate the friendship between the two nations, to mark 100 years since the U.S. Declaration of Independence, and to celebrate the victory of the Union (and the abolition of slavery) in the American Civil War.
  • Location: Liberty Island, New York Harbor (formerly called Bedloe's Island).

2. Etymological Research: Origin and Meaning of the Name

Liberty

The word is derived from the Old French Liberté and the Latin Libertas (the Roman goddess of freedom, upon whom the statue's figure is based). It symbolizes political and legal liberation from subjugation.

Official Name: Liberty Enlightening the World

The person who gave the statue its official name, "Liberty Enlightening the World" (French: La Liberté éclairant le monde), was its creator and mastermind: Édouard René de Laboulaye, along with the sculptor himself, Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi.

Laboulaye was a jurist, political activist, and president of the French Anti-Slavery Society, and he was the one who conceived the idea of the gift. Bartholdi was the one who translated the idea into physical design and the final name.

Reason for the Name:

The name "Liberty Enlightening the World" was chosen for two deep ideological and historical reasons:

  • Republican Ideals and Admiration for the U.S. ("Liberty"): Laboulaye and Bartholdi were French republicans living under the dictatorial rule of Napoleon III. Laboulaye saw the United States as proof that a democracy and a free republic could survive and succeed (especially after the U.S. survived the bloody Civil War and finally abolished slavery in 1865). The word "Liberty" represented this victory and the absolute end of slavery in the U.S.
  • A Message to the Old World ("Enlightening the World"): The name was chosen to send a message to Americans, but primarily to the leaders and peoples of Europe (the "World"). The raised torch was not meant to be just an illuminating lantern, but a philosophical beacon. It was intended to broadcast the "light of reason," democracy, and human rights from a free America back across the ocean to European countries still mired under monarchical rule, tyranny, or oppression. The light symbolizes the Enlightenment era—the spread of knowledge, law, and justice that should guide all nations.

3. The People Behind the Symbol

Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi - The Sculptor

Etymology:
  • Frédéric: From Germanic (Frithuric), combining frithu (peace) and ric (ruler) - "peaceful ruler".
  • Bartholdi: From an old Germanic source, a derivative of the name Bartholomew.

Lifespan: 1834 – 1904.

Childhood: Born in Colmar, Alsace, France. Educated in Paris and absorbed the ideas of the French Republic and human rights. He was deeply influenced by colossal (giant) Egyptian sculpture after visiting Egypt.

Achievements: The sculptor who conceived and designed the statue's outer shell, face, and body. Bartholdi invested nearly two decades of his life in promoting, fundraising, and designing the Statue of Liberty. He pushed the project obsessively, crossing the ocean many times to convince American politicians to provide the island for the statue and build the pedestal. (According to rumor, the Lady's face is modeled after his mother, Charlotte).

Gustave Eiffel - The Skeleton Engineer

Etymology:
  • Gustave: From Old Swedish (Götstaf), possibly "staff of the gods" or a title of support.
  • Eiffel: His family immigrated from the Eifel mountain region in Germany and adopted the region's name.

Lifespan: 1832 – 1923.

Childhood: Born in Dijon, France to a family with German roots. From a young age, he showed exceptional talent for mathematics and visual sciences, completing his engineering and chemistry studies at the "École Centrale des Arts et Manufactures," one of the most prestigious engineering institutions in France.

Achievements: Became one of the greatest metal and bridge engineers in history. After the original engineer of the statue (Viollet-le-Duc) died, Eiffel was hired to solve a massive problem: how do you hold up a thin, giant copper shell (over 46 meters tall) against the hurricane winds of New York Harbor? Eiffel designed a flexible iron skeleton (a central spine that branches into smaller frames) allowing the copper shell to "move and breathe" in the wind and varying temperatures without cracking. A few years later, he applied similar principles when building the famous tower bearing his name in Paris.

4. Anatomy and Engineering: The Green Secret and Full Technical Specs

The Anatomical Symbolism of the Lady:

  • Why is the statue green? Originally, the statue was a bright copper color (like a penny). After about two decades of exposure to rain and salty sea air, the copper oxidized and developed a green Patina layer that protects the underlying metal from further corrosion.
  • The Torch: A symbol of enlightenment. The original damaged torch was replaced in 1986 with a new torch covered in pure 24-karat gold leaf, which reflects sunlight during the day and is illuminated by floodlights at night.
  • The Crown: The crown features 7 rays, symbolizing the 7 continents and 7 seas, emphasizing the universal message of the statue.
  • The Tablet (Tabula Ansata): The statue holds a tablet resembling a book of laws, engraved with the date: JULY IV MDCCLXXVI (July 4, 1776, the date of the Declaration of Independence), connecting the ideal of liberty to the American nation.
  • The Broken Chains: Often missed, but at the feet of the statue lies a broken chain, and her right foot is raised as if she is stepping forward away from slavery (a symbol of the abolition of slavery).

Technical Specifications of the Statue Itself (Liberty Figure):

  • Height: From the tip of the torch to the base of the statue's feet - 46 meters (151 feet and 1 inch).
  • Total Weight: 225 tons.
  • Copper Shell: The statue is wrapped in 300 copper plates. The total weight of the copper is 31 tons (62,000 pounds).
  • Copper Thickness: Incredibly thin – only 2.37 millimeters (roughly the thickness of two US pennies stacked together).
  • Internal Skeleton: Designed by Gustave Eiffel, weighing 125 tons, built from wrought iron, its purpose is to provide flexibility to the statue. It allows the statue to sway up to 7.6 cm (3 inches) and the torch to sway up to 12.7 cm (5 inches) in winds of up to 50 mph.
  • The Crown: Consists of 7 rays (representing the 7 continents and 7 oceans) and features 25 windows (symbolizing gemstones). Each ray in the crown is 2.7 meters long and weighs about 68 kg.
  • The Tablet (Tabula Ansata): Its length is 7.19 meters and width is 4.14 meters.
  • Face: The length of the face is about 2.4 meters, the distance from eye to eye is 76 cm, and the length of the nose is 1.37 meters. Her index finger is 2.4 meters long.

Technical Specifications of the Pedestal (Base):

  • Pedestal Height: 27 meters (89 feet). The pedestal sits on a star-shaped base which is an old fort wall.
  • Star Base Height (Fort Wood): 20 meters (65 feet).
  • Total Height (From ground to torch tip): 93 meters (305 feet and 1 inch).
  • Materials (Pedestal): Built from over 27,000 tons of concrete, faced with bricks and clad in Goshen granite brought from Connecticut. It was the largest concrete pour in the world at the time. The engineer who designed the American base was General Charles Pomeroy Stone, and the architect was Richard Morris Hunt.

5. Cultural Impact & Significance: Symbol of Immigration

The original purpose of the statue was to mark a French-American republican event. But its location created a new reality:

  • The Golden Door of Immigrants: Because the statue stood at the entrance to New York Harbor, it was the first thing seen by millions of weary European immigrants arriving on ships to Ellis Island. For them, the statue did not symbolize American history, but their hope for a new beginning and a life free from poverty and tyranny.
  • The Poem by Emma Lazarus (The New Colossus): This meaning was ultimately solidified thanks to a poem written by Jewish-American poet Emma Lazarus in 1883, which is now engraved on a bronze plaque in the pedestal. The most famous lines invite the poor immigrants:

    "Give me your tired, your poor,
    Your huddled masses yearning to breathe free...
    I lift my lamp beside the golden door!"
