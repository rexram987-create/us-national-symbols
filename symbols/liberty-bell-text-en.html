The Liberty Bell Inscriptions

The Liberty Bell Inscriptions

Cast in 1753

Despite its name, the word "Liberty Bell" does not appear on the bell at all. The text cast into the exterior of the bronze bell consists of three main parts, placed there by the founders in Philadelphia.

Top Ring: The Biblical Verse

"PROCLAIM LIBERTY THROUGHOUT ALL THE LAND UNTO ALL THE INHABITANTS THEREOF LEV. XXV. X."

Source: The Book of Leviticus, Chapter 25, Verse 10.

Chosen by the Pennsylvania Provincial Assembly in 1751, this verse refers to the biblical "Jubilee Year," a time when slaves and prisoners are to be freed. It was this specific inscription that led Abolitionists in the 1830s to adopt the bell as their symbol and rename it the "Liberty Bell."

Middle Ring: The Assembly's Order

BY ORDER OF THE ASSEMBLY OF THE PROVINCE OF PENSYLVANIA FOR THE STATE HOUSE IN PHILADA

Historical Spelling Note: Notice that "Pennsylvania" is spelled with only one "N" (Pensylvania). While this looks like a typo today, spelling was not fully standardized in the 18th century, and this spelling was acceptable at the time (it also appears in the original US Constitution). Additionally, "Philada" was a standard contemporary abbreviation for Philadelphia.

Bottom Ring: The Founders & Year

PASS AND STOW
PHILADA
MDCCLIII

Meaning:
John Pass and John Stow, the two local Philadelphia foundry workers who recast the bell after it cracked on its first test ring.
Philadelphia.
1753 (in Roman numerals).
